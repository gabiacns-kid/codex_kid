# Local Restaurant Google Maps Ads for Foreign Tourists

Use this reference for Korean restaurants or local stores that want Google Ads to attract foreign tourists, especially when they do not have a website and must use Google Business Profile or Google Maps as the landing destination.

## Core Judgment

Do not frame the strategy as only "run multilingual keywords." For tourist-heavy restaurants, the conversion path is usually:

1. Search or Maps discovery
2. Business Profile check
3. Menu, photos, reviews, opening hours, distance
4. Direction click, call click, or save
5. Store visit

Therefore, Google Business Profile readiness is as important as Google Ads setup.

## Primary Media Fit

Recommended first priority:

- Google Search campaign with location assets
- Google Maps local search exposure
- Landing to Google Business Profile / Maps listing

Use Performance Max with store goals only if budget and asset readiness are sufficient. Do not recommend broad Display or YouTube first for small local restaurants.

## Business Profile Checklist

Before launch:

- Claim and verify the Business Profile.
- Confirm restaurant name, address, pin, opening hours, holiday hours, phone number.
- Add strong food photos, interior/exterior photos, menu photos, and short videos if possible.
- Add menu, price range, reservation or contact route if available.
- Add English description first; add Japanese/Chinese copy if possible.
- Review category and attributes such as restaurant type, dining options, Wi-Fi, group seating, reservation, and payment if available.
- Review recent foreign-language reviews and respond where possible.

## Language and Keyword Strategy

Prioritize by expected tourist demand and search behavior:

1. English
2. Japanese
3. Chinese, if menu/profile support is ready
4. Spanish only as a small test unless there is evidence of meaningful Spanish-speaking demand

Language targeting is not the same as translating keywords. Google may use query language, user settings, browsing signals, and other language signals. For Search, ads can target one or multiple languages, and multilingual users may see ads in a language Google believes they understand.

Keyword themes:

- near-me intent: shabu shabu near me, restaurant near Hongdae, hot pot near me
- area intent: Hongdae restaurant, Hongdae shabu shabu, Hongik Univ food
- cuisine intent: Korean hot pot, shabu shabu, all you can eat, buffet, Korean restaurant
- tourist intent: dinner in Hongdae, best restaurant Hongdae, group restaurant Hongdae
- value and experience intent: affordable buffet in Hongdae, good value restaurant Hongdae, clean restaurant Hongdae, friendly staff restaurant Hongdae, restaurant with nice view Hongdae
- question/natural-language intent: where to eat in Hongdae, where can I eat shabu shabu in Hongdae, what is a good buffet near Hongdae, best place for dinner near Hongik University
- language variations: Japanese and Chinese equivalents only if ad copy and profile content support them

## Broad Match / Expanded Search

Broad match can be useful for foreign-tourist local restaurant campaigns because travelers often search in natural language and use varied expressions such as "where to eat near Hongdae" or "good buffet with view in Hongdae." Google's broad match can match related searches beyond the exact keyword and may consider recent search activity, landing page and asset content, and other keywords in the ad group.

Use broad match cautiously:

- Start with phrase/exact match for core visit-intent keywords.
- Add limited broad match groups for discovery only.
- Review search term reports weekly.
- Add negatives for irrelevant informational searches, recipes, jobs, unrelated cities, or non-visit intent.
- Keep broad match budget smaller until the query quality is confirmed.
- Smart Bidding is recommended by Google for broad match, but if there is no website conversion or store-visit conversion, evaluate broad match by CTR, CPC, search-term quality, and campaign-level interaction signals rather than promising conversion optimization.

## Location Strategy

For a physical restaurant, focus on people actually in or near the area, not people overseas who only show interest in Seoul.

Recommended:

- Radius around Hongdae / Hongik University Station / Hapjeong / Yeonnam / Sangsu.
- Use presence targeting or carefully review default presence-or-interest traffic.
- Use location assets and measure direction clicks, call clicks, and business detail clicks.
- Daypart around lunch, dinner, weekend, and tourist peak times.

For foreign-tourist campaigns, separate two demand moments:

1. In-market / in-Korea visitors
   - Target people physically in or regularly in Hongdae, Seoul, or nearby tourist areas.
   - Use presence-focused location settings where appropriate.
   - Keywords should include near-me and immediate meal intent.
   - Main KPIs: direction clicks, calls, Maps/Profile interactions.

2. Pre-trip planners
   - Target priority origin markets or use Seoul/Hongdae interest keywords.
   - Keywords should include itinerary and "where to eat in Hongdae" style planning intent.
   - Main KPIs: profile views, menu clicks, saves, route planning signals if available.
   - Keep this budget smaller because store visit attribution is weaker and delayed.

## KPI

Do not judge only by website conversions when no website exists.

Primary KPI:

- Direction clicks
- Call clicks
- Business Profile detail clicks
- Click share and CTR for high-intent restaurant keywords

Secondary KPI:

- Google Business Profile insights
- Search terms by language
- Review growth and foreign-language review signals
- Store visit conversions if the account becomes eligible

Metric availability should be separated:

- Google Ads can report ad clicks, CTR, CPC, search terms, language/campaign segmentation if the account structure separates languages, time/day performance, and location asset interactions when configured.
- Google Business Profile performance can report views, searches, directions, calls, website clicks, messages, bookings if applicable, product views, menu interactions, and offers depending on business/profile setup.
- Some indicators are indirect. Review growth, foreign-language review quality, and actual visits require Business Profile review monitoring or offline store feedback, not only the Google Ads report.

## Client-Facing Positioning

Good wording:

> Because the restaurant does not currently have a website, the campaign should use Google Business Profile and Google Maps as the main landing path. For foreign tourists, the decision is often made by distance, photos, menu clarity, reviews, and directions, so profile optimization must be completed before or alongside advertising. Search ads should start with English and nearby-intent keywords, then expand to Japanese and Chinese after confirming profile and menu readiness.

Avoid:

- Saying Google Maps-only ads can be guaranteed. Google Maps is a serving surface, but ads may also show across Search or eligible local formats depending on campaign setup.
- Expanding many languages without matching ad copy, menu information, photos, and profile content.
- Running broad Display/YouTube first when the objective is immediate store visit.
